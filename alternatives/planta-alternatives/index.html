Five plant care apps, checked September 2026

Planta alternatives

Planta is a good plant care app, and most people who search for a replacement are not leaving because it is bad. They are leaving because of the price, because the features they wanted sit behind Premium, or because the app now needs iOS 18. Here are five apps to consider, with what each one costs and where it falls short.

Last checked: September 14, 2026

Kipos publishes this page, and Kipos is the first app on it. Kipos is not affiliated with or endorsed by Planta AB or any other developer named here. Every price comes from the US App Store listing on the date above. Features and prices change, so check the listing before you decide.

The short answer

If you want a plant record that tracks phase, care history and photos for a small collection, consider Kipos. If your only complaint about Planta is the subscription, Greg keeps care tracking and unlimited plants free. PlantIn is the closest feature match to Planta at a lower yearly price. PictureThis is the identification specialist. Blossom is the simplest reminder app of the five. Planta itself is still the right choice if you want diagnosis, a light meter, a community and twelve languages in one app.

Why people look for a Planta alternative

Planta Premium is $35.99 a year in the US App Store, with a monthly plan at $9.99 and a three-month plan at $19.99. The listing also shows yearly offers from $17.99 to $47.99, which is how Planta tests prices on different users. The free version keeps basic reminders and logging. Identification, the light meter, Dr. Planta diagnosis and unlimited plants need Premium.

The current version also requires iOS 18.0 or later. An iPhone that cannot run iOS 18, such as the iPhone X or 8, can no longer install it.

The third reason is scope. Planta covers identification, diagnosis, light measurement, journaling, shared care and a community. Some people want most of that. Others want one reliable schedule and a record of what happened, and find the rest in the way. The apps below split along that line.

The five apps

  1. 1

    Kipos

    Best for a small garden or plant collection you want a record of, not only reminders for.

    Price
    $9.99/month or $39.99/year; 14-day trial on yearly
    Free
    First plant and its care plan
    Platforms
    iPhone, iOS 15.1 or later
    Languages
    English

    Kipos is a garden tracker for iPhone. You add a plant by scanning it or typing its name, answer a few questions about where it lives (the room, the light, whether the pot drains, when you last watered), and Kipos writes a watering, light and feeding plan for that plant in that spot. The schedule comes from your answers rather than a species default. A pothos in a dim hallway with no drainage hole gets a different interval than the same plant on a bright sill.

    Each plant then has a card: its current growth phase, which you move forward by hand, a dated care history, photos and notes. Completed care earns points, a streak adds a bonus, and every plant averages into one Garden Score. When a day of care is done, Kipos can turn it into a card you can share outside the app. It is made for small gardens: balcony pots, raised beds, a few favorite houseplants.

    Where it wins

    • Care plans built from your room, pot, climate and season, with the interval editable per plant
    • A growth phase on every plant, plus a dated history of what you did
    • Feeding plans with a fertilizer type, NPK ratio and dose for that plant
    • Runs on iOS 15.1, so it installs on phones Planta has dropped
    • No ads and no free-tier nagging; one subscription covers everything

    Where it falls short

    • No plant diagnosis, light meter, community or shared care
    • iPhone and English only; no Android, iPad or other languages
    • Launched September 2026, so there is no track record yet
    • No free tier past the first plant; the trial is 14 days on the yearly plan only

  2. 2

    Greg

    Best for keeping Planta-style care tracking without paying, and asking other growers when something goes wrong.

    Price
    Free; SUPER Greg $29.99/year
    Free
    Care tracking, unlimited plants, identification, community
    Platforms
    iPhone (iOS 16 or later), Android
    Languages
    English

    Greg is the most direct swap if the subscription is your only complaint. Care tracking and unlimited plants are free, and the app builds a watering plan from the plant's species, pot size and the conditions in your home. Its photo identification, called PlantVision, is also free.

    The difference from Planta is the community. Greg has an in-app feed where people post a photo of a struggling plant and other growers answer, usually within a day. Planta answers with Dr. Planta; Greg answers with people. The paid tier, SUPER Greg, adds precision watering, smarter reminders, a light gauge and seasonal adjustments for $29.99 a year, and the listing shows offers from $1.99 to $39.99.

    Where it wins

    • The free tier covers tracking, reminders and unlimited plants, and does not expire
    • An active community that answers plant problems
    • Available on iPhone and Android

    Where it falls short

    • Advice quality depends on who answers; there is no structured diagnosis
    • English only, and the App Store age rating is 16+ because of the chat and user content
    • The best watering features sit in SUPER Greg

    Greg on the App Store · Greg on Google Play

  3. 3

    PlantIn

    Best for people who want Planta's toolset (diagnosis, light meter, care plans) and would rather pay $29.99 a year or buy it once.

    Price
    $29.99/year or $49.99 lifetime
    Free
    Limited; most tools need Premium
    Platforms
    iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch (iOS 16.4 or later), Android
    Languages
    19

    PlantIn covers the most Planta ground of any app here. It identifies plants from a photo, diagnoses diseases, measures light with the camera, calculates watering and writes care plans that include fertilizing and pruning. It also has a mushroom identifier and a paid Ask the Botanist service for questions the app cannot answer.

    The price is the draw. Premium is $29.99 a year, and a lifetime purchase is listed at $49.99, with a $19.99 lifetime offer appearing at times. Planta has no lifetime option. The free tier is thin: the listing does not describe much that works without Premium. The weekly plans, from $6.99 to $17.99, cost far more per month than the yearly plan, so read the paywall before you tap.

    Where it wins

    • Diagnosis, light meter, watering calculator and care plans in one app
    • The lowest yearly price in this list, plus a lifetime option
    • 19 languages, and an Apple Watch app

    Where it falls short

    • Little works for free, and the weekly plans are easy to subscribe to by mistake
    • Botanist answers are a paid service, not part of the basic app
    • Requires iOS 16.4, so it also drops older phones

    PlantIn on the App Store · PlantIn on Google Play

    PictureThis

    Best for naming an unknown plant fast, then reading how to care for it.

    Price
    Pro $39.99/year; family plan $49.99
    Free
    A few identifications, then Pro
    Platforms
    iPhone, iPad (iOS 15 or later), Android
    Languages
    28

    PictureThis is an identifier first. Point the camera at a leaf and it returns a species name in a second or two, with care notes and a diagnosis tool behind it. The developer, Glority, claims more than 400,000 species and 98 percent accuracy.

    As a Planta replacement it is a partial fit. The care reminders exist but are thinner than Planta's, and Pro costs $39.99 a year, which is more than Planta Premium. The 7-day trial converts to a paid subscription unless you cancel. Pick it if identification is the feature you use most and you are willing to keep a simpler tracker alongside it.

    Where it wins

    • Fast identification from a very large species database
    • Diagnosis and care guides on every species page
    • 28 languages

    Where it falls short

    • More expensive than Planta Premium
    • Care scheduling is secondary; the app is built around the scan
    • The free version allows only a handful of identifications before the paywall

    PictureThis on the App Store · PictureThis on Google Play

  5. 5

    Blossom

    Best for a simple, good-looking reminder app for a few houseplants.

    Price
    $59.99 to $79.99/year, $5.99/month, $7.99/week
    Free
    The listing does not say what stays free
    Platforms
    iPhone (iOS 15.4 or later), Android
    Languages
    5

    Blossom identifies more than 30,000 plants from a photo and then builds reminders for watering, fertilizing and repotting based on the plant, its light and the time of year. It has a light meter, a watering calculator that accounts for pot size and a disease identifier. The interface is the cleanest of the five, and it leans on gentle nudges to keep you watering on schedule.

    It is also the most expensive. The yearly plan is listed between $59.99 and $79.99 depending on the offer you see, and the weekly bundle at $7.99 works out to more than $400 a year if left running. If you are leaving Planta over price, Blossom moves you in the wrong direction. If you are leaving because Planta feels like too much app, Blossom is the smaller, calmer option.

    Where it wins

    • Clean, low-effort interface
    • Reminders that account for light and season, plus a pot-size watering calculator
    • Identification, light meter and disease check included

    Where it falls short

    • The highest yearly price here, and a weekly plan that adds up fast
    • Only 5 languages
    • The listing does not describe a growth record or history beyond reminders

    Blossom on the App Store · Blossom on Google Play

How to choose

Start with the one thing Planta did not give you.

  • A free tier that never expires: Greg.
  • Planta's toolset for less money: PlantIn. The $49.99 lifetime purchase costs less than two years of Planta Premium.
  • Faster identification of unknown plants: PictureThis, with a simpler tracker alongside it for reminders.
  • A calmer app with fewer screens: Blossom.

If what you wanted was a record, consider Kipos. A reminder tells you what is due today. A record also shows the phase each plant is in, the photos and notes you kept, and the care that led to this point. Kipos is the only app on this list built around that, and it is also the one with the shortest track record and the narrowest platform support. The 14-day trial on the yearly plan is there so you can decide with your own plants.

Questions people ask

How much does Planta cost in 2026?

In the US App Store, Planta Premium is $9.99 a month, $19.99 for three months or $35.99 a year. The listing also shows yearly prices of $17.99, $39.99 and $47.99, which are offers shown to different users. The download is free.

What is free in Planta?

Basic care reminders and logging. Plant identification, the light meter, Dr. Planta diagnosis and unlimited plants need Premium.

Is there a free Planta alternative?

Greg is the closest. Care tracking, unlimited plants, photo identification and the community are free, and the free tier does not expire. Its paid tier adds precision watering and a light gauge. PlantNet is fully free but is an identification tool for wild plants, not a care app, which is why it is not on this list.

Which alternative is most like Planta?

PlantIn. It has identification, diagnosis, a light meter, a watering calculator and care plans, at $29.99 a year or $49.99 for life. Its free tier is thinner than Planta's.

Which app works on an older iPhone?

Planta now requires iOS 18. Kipos runs on iOS 15.1 or later, PictureThis on iOS 15, Blossom on iOS 15.4, Greg on iOS 16 and PlantIn on iOS 16.4.

Can I move my plants from Planta to another app?

None of these apps advertise an import from Planta. You add each plant again, by scan or by name. In Kipos that also means answering a few questions per plant so the schedule fits the room it is in.

Is Kipos a Planta replacement?

For some people. Kipos covers scheduling, reminders, photos, notes and a growth phase per plant. It does not do diagnosis, light measurement, community or shared care. If you used those in Planta, Kipos will feel like less. If you used Planta as a watering log and wished it kept a better record, Kipos is the closer fit.
